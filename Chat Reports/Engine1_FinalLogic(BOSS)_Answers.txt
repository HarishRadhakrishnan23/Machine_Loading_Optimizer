Your Point 6 — safety stock in a mixed batch on heavy ops

Here's my proposed rule; tell me where it's wrong.

The economics: appending safety-stock pieces to a batch that already has the fixture+locator mounted costs no fixture/locator change — only LOAD_UNLOAD × qty plus CYCLE_TIME × qty of machine time. On a light op (VB02 Cone Oversize) that machine time is small, so riding safety stock along is nearly free and worth doing. On a heavy op (VB03/04/05/06) that per-piece time is large, so those extra pieces can push a committed order past its CDD.

The policy — split, then protect committed work absolutely:

Within each fixture/locator group, split into a committed sub-batch (CDD not null) and a safety sub-batch (CDD null).
Light ops: run them together — safety rides along (cheap, builds stock while the fixture is already up).
Heavy ops (configurable set): schedule the committed sub-batch first. Then attempt to append the safety sub-batch immediately after (zero fixture/locator change) only if doing so delays no committed order — anywhere — past its CDD, accounting for the shared fixture pool. If it would, don't run safety now: release the fixture/locator back to the pool and re-queue the safety sub-batch at the global tail (lowest priority), to be backfilled later only into genuine idle windows where its fixture is free.
Net effect: a committed order is never delayed by safety stock on a heavy op, but safety stock still gets made whenever it's actually free to make.

To wire this I'd add config: heavy_operations = ["VB03","VB04","VB05","VB06"] and protect_committed_over_safety = true, so the planner controls the heavy-op set and the rule is auditable.
